### Day 3 · Mon 7 Sep · Spitalfields, the City, Bankside
> The one day that isn't the West End. You open at a market in the East, walk a straight line south through the old City, cross the river for a Michelin lunch, and finish in front of a Rothko. Three Londons in a day, nearly all of it on foot.

- 🛍️ **10:00am: Old Spitalfields Market.** Open Mondays 10:00-20:00, which is precisely why the day starts here: it is the market Borough cannot be today. Traders under a Victorian glass roof, the Kitchens street-food hall firing from 11:00. Browse, don't eat. Lunch is the event.
- 🚶 **11:15am: walk south through the City.** Twenty-five unhurried minutes and the best free architecture in London: **Leadenhall Market's** painted Victorian arcade (Diagon Alley in the first Potter film), the Gherkin directly overhead, then **the Monument** to Wren's Great Fire. You come out at London Bridge with Tower Bridge over your left shoulder.
- 🍴 **12:30pm: Oma, 3 Bedale Street.** Oma is Greek for "raw," and the crudo bar is the first thing you pass: open with the seabass crudo under ladolemono and crisp fried leek (£13). Taramasalata (£6) with the bread the moment you sit, studded with pickled cucumber and crushed carob biscuit. Then the spanakopita gratin (£15) and the lobster bisque börek (£12), the one everybody leaves talking about.
- 🚶 **2:15pm: the Bankside walk.** Twelve minutes west along the water: Shakespeare's Globe, the Millennium Bridge, St Paul's sitting square in the frame across the river.
- 👀 **2:30pm: Tate Modern.** Permanent collection free. Turbine Hall first, then straight up to the level 10 terrace of the Blavatnik Building, also free, and the best no-ticket view in London: St Paul's and the whole bend of the river. It makes the Shard's £32 look silly.
- 🛏️ **~4:30pm: back to the hotel.** You will have walked most of the City by now. An hour off your feet is not optional.
- 🍴 **7:00pm: St. JOHN, Smithfield.** The room that reset British cooking: whitewashed walls, no decoration, a menu that changes daily and never apologises. Roast bone marrow and parsley salad is the dish the last thirty years of London restaurants are a footnote to. Order the madeleines when you sit down; they are baked to order and take fifteen minutes. Supper runs 6:00-10:30pm Mon-Sat.

> ⭐ **The Move:** the bone marrow at St. JOHN. Of everything on this trip, it is the one plate that could not be eaten anywhere else in the world.
> ⚠️ **Heads up:** Borough Market is closed every Monday (Tue-Fri 10-5, Sat 9-5, Sun 10-4). Oma itself stays open Monday 12:00-15:00, so lunch is safe, but there is no market at Borough today. Spitalfields covers it.
> 🎯 **Book it:** two for Monday. **Oma**, table for 4, 12:30pm (reminder set 7 Aug). **St. JOHN**, table for 3-4, 7:00pm, online, minimum two diners.

### Day 4 · Tue 8 Sep · Soho, St James's, Mayfair
> The last full day, and the tightest geography of the trip: Persian charcoal in Soho, the two best martinis in London a few streets apart, then a late table in Mayfair. You could walk the whole day in twenty minutes of total effort.

- 🍴 **Lunch: Berenjak Soho, 27 Romilly Street, table for 3.** Booked by Louise. ✅ Charcoal-grilled Persian off a tiny open kitchen. The kabab barg and the mast-o khiar, and do not skip the bread pulled straight off the taftoon oven at the pass.
- 🍸 **~5:00pm: Duke's Bar, St James's Place.** The martini is wheeled to your table and mixed in front of you, gin straight from the freezer, no ice, no dilution. Ian Fleming drank here and lifted "shaken, not stirred" from the room. There is a two-martini house limit and it exists for a reason.
- 🍸 **~6:30pm: The Connaught Bar, Carlos Place.** Eight minutes' walk north. The trolley again, this time with a choice of five bitters and a lemon twist cut at the table. Widely rated the best bar in the world, and it earns it.
- 🍴 **Late: Gymkhana, 42 Albemarle Street.** Two Michelin stars, colonial-club dining room, the loudest and best-dressed Indian food in London. The wild muntjac biryani is the signature; the kid goat methi keema with brain naan is the one people fly in for.

> ⭐ **The Move:** Duke's → the Connaught. Two of the greatest cocktail rooms on earth, eight minutes apart, in the right order.
> ⚠️ **Heads up:** this is a genuinely heavy day. Lunch, two Duke's martinis (they are near-neat gin), a Connaught round on top, and then a two-star tasting-style dinner. Eat properly at Berenjak, keep the Connaught to one, and push the Gymkhana booking to 9:00pm or later so the evening has room to breathe.
> 🎯 **Book it:** **Gymkhana**, table for 3-4, late on the 8th. It is the hardest reservation on this trip; go now. **The Connaught** takes bookings and should have one. **Duke's** is walk-in only, so arrive close to 5:00pm opening.
